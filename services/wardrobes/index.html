What you get

How we build wardrobes & storage

The specifics that separate work that lasts a decade from work that looks fine for two years.

Sliding, hinged or walk-in

Sliding for tight rooms where a swing door would block circulation, hinged for full access, walk-in where the floor plan allows a dedicated dressing zone.

Internals designed to your inventory

Sari drawers, trouser pull-outs, tie racks, valet rods and shoe pull-outs — specified after we count what you own, not from a catalogue default.

Full-height, loft included

Wardrobes run floor to ceiling with an integrated loft. That dead 18 inches above a standard wardrobe is where your luggage should live.

Mirror and lighting integration

Full-length mirror on a shutter or a pull-out mirror panel, with sensor-activated internal LED so you can see the back of a dark wardrobe.

Locker and safe provision

Concealed locker compartments and safe housing built into the carcass, not bolted on afterwards.

Anti-borer and moisture treatment

Borer-treated ply as standard, with a moisture barrier on any wall-abutting panel — critical in ground-floor and north-facing NCR bedrooms.

Sliding if the room is under 11 feet wide or the wardrobe faces the bed with less than 900mm clearance. Hinged if you want to see the full wardrobe at once and want to save 20–30% on cost. Sliding loses you about 8% of internal depth to the track mechanism — worth knowing before you decide.

Internals over finish, every time. Drawers, pull-outs, correct hanging heights and a proper loft change how the wardrobe works daily; the shutter finish only changes how it looks. If something has to give, take a simpler finish and keep the internals.

Modular units can be dismantled and re-installed, though you should expect some material loss and a re-fitting charge. For rented homes we usually recommend a freestanding modular system instead — we will flag this at design stage if you tell us the home is rented.
